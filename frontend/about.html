Technical Documentation

AgroRisk Advisor is a rule-based deterministic engine combining validated agricultural datasets to assess climate and market risk for crops.

System Architecture

The application is designed as an offline-first "thick client" where the rules engine executes entirely within the browser. This eliminates API latency, guarantees privacy, and allows the tool to function even on intermittent rural networks.

  1. User Input: The user selects their district, season, irrigation type, and optional previous crop.
  2. Offline JS Rule Engine: The client-side logic processes the input against pre-loaded geographical profiles and crop parameters.
  3. Knowledge Datasets: The data points are validated against FAO standards, IMD district normals, and ICAR advisory models.
  4. Explainable Recommendation: The final output presents a risk-scored ranking with deterministic reasoning logic available for every crop.

Scientific Advisory Protocols (v7.5)

Our calculation engines follow primary scientific principles to ensure conservative and safe advice:

  • Liebig’s Law of the Minimum (Threshold-Based): Aggregate suitability is governed by the most limiting environmental factor once it crosses a critical threshold (65% risk). This ensures hard constraints dominate when conditions become high-risk.
  • Multiplicative Reliability (Geometric Mean): Confidence is calculated as $(\text{DataQuality} \times \text{Stability} \times \text{Uncertainty})^{1/3}$. This is a Model Reliability Indicator, not a statistical probability.
  • Effective Rainfall: Water availability is adjusted for soil texture efficiency (Infiltration/Runoff) based on FAO standards. Terrain and slope are excluded from the current prototype but flagged for future calibration.
  • Non-Linear Penalty Curves: Environmental stress is modeled using power-law penalties ($Risk \propto Deficit^{1.5}$) to capture the sudden threshold collapse typical in tropical agriculture.

Data Sources Used

The rules engine relies on public governmental and international agricultural data standards. The datasets currently driving the engine include:

  • FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56: Provides baseline crop evapotranspiration coefficients and minimum/maximum mm water requirements.
  • IMD District Rainfall Statistics: Indian Meteorological Department normal datasets provide average annual and seasonal (Kharif, Rabi, Summer) precipitation probabilities.
  • ICAR Crop Calendars & Advisories: Indian Council of Agricultural Research guidelines inform seasonal compatibility, duration bounds, and monoculture/rotation penalties.
  • Agmarknet / PIB: Market commodity spot data is used to tag historical high-volatility crash risks for specific crops like onions, tomatoes, and potatoes.

System Limitations

Heuristic Model Only

This tool provides heuristic risk indications based on broad geographical averages. It does NOT replace the necessity for on-ground evaluation.

  • Soil Health: Does NOT replace physical soil testing. Variables like pH, EC, and micro-nutrient profiles are not currently factored.
  • Sub-district Micro-climates: Uses district-level rainfall averages (IMD normals), which may not accurately reflect hyper-local rain shadow zones.
  • Market Pricing: Does NOT forecast real-time market prices or account for specific mandi trends; volatility is marked heuristically based on historical variance.
  • Expert Consultation: Does NOT replace on-ground agricultural experts or specific state extension advisories.
