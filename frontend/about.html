Technical Documentation

Farmlytics is a rule-based deterministic engine combining validated agricultural datasets to assess climate, soil, irrigation, and market risk for crops.

System Architecture

The application is designed with a decoupled architecture where the Python backend processes rules against pre-loaded geographical profiles and crop parameters.

  1. User Input: Select district, season, irrigation type, and optional previous crop.
  2. Python Rule Engine: Processes inputs against environmental rules and district statistics.
  3. Knowledge Datasets: Data points validated against FAO standards, IMD district normals, and ICAR models.
  4. Explainable Recommendation: Outputs risk-scored crop rankings with natural-language reasoning.

Scientific Advisory Protocols

Our calculation engines follow primary scientific principles to ensure conservative and safe advice:

  • Liebig’s Law of the Minimum: Aggregate suitability is governed by the most limiting environmental factor once it crosses a critical threshold.
  • Multiplicative Reliability: Confidence is calculated across data quality, stability, and environmental variability.
  • Effective Rainfall: Water availability is adjusted for soil texture efficiency based on FAO standards.
  • Non-Linear Penalty Curves: Environmental stress is modeled using power-law penalties to capture threshold collapses in tropical agriculture.

Data Sources Used

The rules engine relies on public governmental and international agricultural data standards:

  • FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56: Baseline evapotranspiration coefficients and mm water requirements.
  • IMD District Rainfall Statistics: Indian Meteorological Department normal datasets for annual and seasonal precipitation.
  • ICAR Crop Calendars & Advisories: Indian Council of Agricultural Research guidelines for seasonal compatibility and crop rotations.
  • Agmarknet / PIB: Market commodity data used to tag historical volatility and MSP values.

System Limitations

Heuristic Model Only

This tool provides heuristic risk indications based on geographical averages. It does NOT replace physical soil testing or expert field evaluation.
